**APPENDIX A**

# Mulligan’s Rules

THE following forty rules are not a complete code of combat flying. No such code exists. These are simply the rules I have found myself repeating, over a long career, to pupils, wingmen, and my own muttering self in the cockpit. I offer them for whatever use you may find in them.

A pilot who internalizes these rules will not thereby become an ace. A pilot who ignores them will become a statistic.

1. If you can see them, assume they can see you.

2. Never trust a quiet scope.

3. The shot you do not take cannot miss. The shot you do not take also cannot hit.

4. Your wingman is always more important than your target.

5. Fuel is time. Time is everything.

6. Fly the ship you are in, not the ship you wish you had.

7. A tight grip is a dead grip.

8. The instruments lie. The canopy does not.

9. Every turn costs you something. Know what it costs before you make it.

10. The enemy pilot is not stupid. Assume they are as skilled as you are, until proven otherwise.

11. A pilot who has stopped being afraid has stopped paying attention.

12. The merge is five seconds. The rest of the fight is what you do with those five seconds.

13. Never fly a straight line for more than thirty seconds. A straight line is a prediction.

14. Three bursts per engagement. If you are firing more, you are not flying right.

15. The pilot who breaks first, in a turning fight, usually loses. The pilot who tries not to break, also loses. Learn the difference.

16. Check your six every ten seconds. Your brain will forget. Your eyes should not.

17. If your ship is damaged, the damage is worse than you think.

18. If you are lost, admit it. Nobody has ever died from asking for a vector home.

19. Altitude is a bank account. Do not spend it on whims.

20. The vertical is a gift. Use it.

21. Never follow an enemy into a descent they have chosen. They are taking you somewhere they want you to be.

22. A good wingman is worth more than a better ship.

23. Your crew chief is the second most important person in your life. Act accordingly.

24. The mission is to come home. The kills are a side effect.

25. A pilot who boasts about their kills has not killed enough to understand what it costs.

26. The first shot announces that you are a pilot who shoots. Take it, even if it misses.

27. If you are firewall on the throttle for more than sixty seconds, you have lost the fight. You just do not know it yet.

28. Every dogfight ends. Make sure you end it rather than being ended by it.

29. Trust your training more than your instincts. Your instincts evolved for a different planet.

30. The pilot who thinks the enemy is predictable is about to be surprised.

31. Never assume a damaged enemy is a dead enemy, until you have watched them stop flying.

32. The best maneuver is the one the enemy did not expect. The second best is the one you have practiced until it is automatic.

33. A rookie who asks too many questions is annoying. A rookie who asks too few questions is dead.

34. The briefing is not a lecture. It is a working meeting. Participate accordingly.

35. When you break contact, do not relax until you are on the pad.

36. A pilot who fights a medal is not a pilot. They are a character. Do not be a character.

37. Old pilots are old for a reason. Listen to them, even when they are wrong.

38. Young pilots are young for a reason. Teach them, even when they are annoying.

39. Fly well. Come home. Make the ones who loved you proud.

40. If you are reading these rules on a posting in the ready room, a copy has been made without my permission, and I am glad it was.
